## Part A: How Earth Formed (Target 2.1)

**1.** About 4.6 billion years ago, the Sun, Earth and the asteroids all formed from

- A. pieces that broke off the Sun
- B. the same spinning cloud of gas and dust (the solar nebula)
- C. an older planet that exploded
- D. comets from another galaxy

**2.** Early Earth grew as small rocky pieces collided and stuck together. This is called *accretion*. Early Earth got hot enough to melt, but many small asteroids never melted. What is the best explanation?

- A. Earth was farther from the Sun than the asteroids were.
- B. Asteroids are made only of ice.
- C. Earth was much larger, so it built up more heat and held on to it longer.
- D. Asteroids formed billions of years after Earth.

**3.** Iron meteorites are made mostly of iron and nickel. They are pieces of the metal cores of broken asteroids. What does this suggest about Earth?

- A. When early Earth melted, heavy iron sank to the center to form a core.
- B. Earth's surface is mostly iron.
- C. Earth formed only from iron meteorites.
- D. Earth does not have a core.

## Part B: Radioactive Clocks (Target 2.2)

**4.** Which of these is the best definition of *half-life*?

- A. Half of a rock's total age
- B. The time it takes for a rock to melt
- C. The time it takes for half of a radioactive sample to decay
- D. The time it takes for all of the atoms in a sample to decay

**5.** Which of these can change how fast a radioactive element decays?

- A. Heat
- B. Pressure
- C. Chemical reactions
- D. None of these; the decay rate stays the same

**6.** Potassium-40 decays to argon-40 with a half-life of 1.25 billion years. A volcanic rock has 25% of its original potassium-40 left. How old is the rock?

- A. 0.625 billion years
- B. 1.25 billion years
- C. 2.5 billion years
- D. 5.0 billion years

**7.** A scientist wants to find the age of a meteorite that may be about 4.5 billion years old. Which radioactive clock should she use?

- A. Carbon-14 (half-life 5,730 years), because it is the most common clock
- B. Carbon-14, because a short half-life works for any age
- C. Any of them would work equally well
- D. Uranium-238 (half-life 4.47 billion years), because its long half-life fits very old ages
