### LT 3.1: P-waves vs S-waves (DOK 1)

**Q1. Multiple choice · self-graded · align to ESS2-3.1**
Which type of seismic wave can travel through solids **and** liquids?
- A. P-wave ✅
- B. S-wave
- C. Neither wave can travel through liquids
- D. Only surface waves

**Q2. Multiple choice · self-graded · align to ESS2-3.1**
An S-wave reaches a layer of liquid rock. What happens to the S-wave?
- A. It speeds up.
- B. It turns into a P-wave.
- C. It stops. S-waves cannot travel through liquids. ✅
- D. It passes through without changing.

**Q3. Multiple choice · self-graded · align to ESS2-3.1**
An earthquake happens far away. Which wave reaches a seismograph station first, and why?
- A. The S-wave, because it shakes the ground side to side.
- B. Both waves arrive at the same time.
- C. The S-wave, because it travels only through solids.
- D. The P-wave, because it is the fastest seismic wave. ✅

### LT 3.2: Seismic data reveals layers (DOK 3)

Shared data table for Q4 and Q5 (paste it above Q4 as a passage/stimulus):

> Seismograph stations recorded one earthquake. Distance is measured in degrees around Earth from the earthquake.
>
> | Station | Distance from earthquake | P-waves recorded? | S-waves recorded? |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | 1 | 30° | Yes | Yes |
> | 2 | 80° | Yes | Yes |
> | 3 | 120° | No | No |
> | 4 | 160° | Yes | No |

**Q4. Multiple choice · self-graded · align to ESS2-3.2**
Station 4 recorded P-waves but **no** S-waves. Which conclusion does this data best support?
- A. Earth's crust is liquid.
- B. Part of Earth's core is liquid, because S-waves cannot pass through it. ✅
- C. All of Earth's interior is solid.
- D. The earthquake made only P-waves.

**Q5. Free response · teacher-graded (4 pts) · align to ESS2-3.2**
Use the data table. Explain what the shadow zone tells scientists about Earth's interior. In your answer:
1. Make a claim about which layer is liquid.
2. Use at least **two** pieces of data from the table as evidence.
3. Explain your reasoning: how do P-waves and S-waves act in a liquid?

Rubric:

| Score | What the answer shows |
| --- | --- |
| 4 | Correct claim (outer core is liquid). Cites 2+ data points (e.g., no S-waves at 160°; no direct waves at 120°; both waves at 30° and 80°). Explains S-waves cannot pass through liquid and P-waves bend (refract) at the core, making the shadow zone. |
| 3 | Correct claim and 2 data points. Reasoning is mostly right but leaves out the P-wave bending or is unclear. |
| 2 | Correct claim with 1 data point, or 2 data points with weak or missing reasoning. |
| 1 | Claim only (e.g., "the core is liquid") with no data, or data with a wrong claim. |
| 0 | No answer or not related to the question. |

Sample 4-point answer: *The outer core is liquid. Station 4 at 160° got P-waves but no S-waves, and Station 3 at 120° got no waves at all. S-waves cannot travel through liquid, so they stop at the outer core. P-waves can pass through but they bend when they hit the core, so they miss some stations and make a shadow zone.*

### LT 3.5: Model mantle convection (DOK 3)

**Q12. Multiple choice · self-graded · align to ESS2-3.5**
In a model of mantle convection, rock near the core gets heated. What happens to it next?
- A. It becomes more dense and sinks.
- B. It becomes less dense and rises toward the surface. ✅
- C. It melts completely and becomes the outer core.
- D. It stays in place because the mantle is solid.

**Q13. Free response · teacher-graded (4 pts) · align to ESS2-3.5**
Draw a model (or describe one in words) of convection in Earth's mantle. Your model must:
1. Label the heat source (the core).
2. Show where warm rock rises and where cool rock sinks, with arrows that make a loop.
3. Explain in 2-3 sentences how this loop moves heat from the inside of Earth toward the surface **and** how it carries rock up to the surface and back down.

Rubric:

| Score | What the answer shows |
| --- | --- |
| 4 | Labeled heat source; arrows form a complete loop (rise near core, move along under crust, sink when cool). Explains warm rock is less dense and rises, cool rock is denser and sinks, which moves heat outward and cycles rock between the interior and the surface. |
| 3 | Complete loop and correct rise/sink, but the explanation leaves out either density or how rock is cycled. |
| 2 | Shows rising and sinking but the loop is incomplete or unlabeled, or the explanation has one error (e.g., says the mantle is liquid). |
| 1 | Some correct idea (e.g., "hot things rise") but no working model or explanation. |
| 0 | No answer or not related to the question. |

Sample 4-point answer: *Heat from the core warms rock at the bottom of the mantle. The warm rock is less dense, so it slowly rises. Near the crust it cools, gets denser, and sinks back down, making a loop. This loop carries heat from deep inside Earth up toward the surface, and it brings rock up to the surface and pulls old rock back down.*
