Work through the steps in order. Each step matches one line in your notebook. Use evidence from the specimen photo, the hand lens, and the specimen tag.

1

Origin: lava flow or tephra?

Lava flow material hardened as part of a flow of molten rock moving across the ground. Pyroclastic material (tephra) was thrown into the air by an eruption, as liquid blobs or as solid pieces, and then landed. The "Collected from" line on the tag is your best clue.

2

Texture 1: grain size

Look through the hand lens. Pick the one texture that describes the main body of the rock.

3

Texture 2: holes

Holes in a volcanic rock are vesicles. They are gas bubbles that were frozen in place as the lava cooled. If the hand lens note says holes make up about 10% of the rock or more, choose vesicular. If there are no holes, or only a few, choose none.

4

Composition and rock name

Color is a helpful hint, but it can fool you. Volcanic glass is dark even when it is felsic. Use the silica (SiO₂) percentage on the tag to decide.

6

Name the volcanic material

  1. If it is from a lava flow, choose one of the four lava flow materials.
  2. If it is tephra and shows a shape that formed while it was soft and flying (twisted, stretched, splatted, cracked crust), it is a bomb or agglutinate.
  3. If it is tephra and mostly holes, it is pumice, scoria, or reticulite, whatever its size.
  4. Otherwise, name it by size: ash, lapilli, or volcanic block.
